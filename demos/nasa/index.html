How a report gets written

01

It wakes up early

Half an hour before each deadline, at 7:30 and 3:30.

02

It reads what you already have

The B/L Sheet and the day's email for the vessels in the pilot. Nothing else.

03

It pulls out the facts

Deliveries, crew movements, flights, hotels, dates. Each one traced back to the email it came from.

04

It writes the draft

In your format, into the operator's Outlook drafts, flagging anything it could not confirm.

05

It records what happened

Which reports were drafted, what was flagged, how long preparation took.

06

A person sends it

Your operator reads the draft, fixes what needs fixing, and decides. Always.

Four things it will never do

Never sendsNo message reaches a customer unless one of your people chooses to send it.
Never paysIt cannot submit a payment or move funds. Not Pay.gov, not the CBP vessel fee portal, not a bank.
Never filesENOA/D, AMS, VECS and customs entries can be prepared. A person submits them.
Never guessesAnything it cannot confirm is flagged in the draft, where your operator will see it.

All data on this page is invented for illustration. The vessels, file numbers, masters, IMO numbers and times are made up to show the layout. The ports are real Gulf ports you serve. Nothing here is a measurement of NASA's operation.
